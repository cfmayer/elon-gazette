The Morning Picture

A thank-you stop in Shanghai, and a launch-site pitch from Manila

Elon Musk closed a 24-hour trip to China by flying from Shenzhen to Gigafactory Shanghai on Thursday to thank Tesla's China and Asia-Pacific teams in person, his first visit to the plant since 2023. On Friday in Shenzhen, Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said his government has begun talks with SpaceX about launch sites on the western edge of the Pacific. Back in Texas, Tesla opened public rides in its steering-wheel-free Cybercab in Dallas, the second city after Austin. Musk said Tesla's AI, running on the AI4 computer in its cars, can now reach Torment II in Diablo; called the Senate's 62-33 permitting bill "promising"; cheered the Boring Company's 40-year deal for a Loop station at Nashville's airport; and said a whole company can be built from Grok Bots.

I · Tesla

"I wanted to come to Shanghai to thank the Asia-Pacific and China teams"

Musk's first visit to Giga Shanghai since 2023 came at the end of a day in Shenzhen with the US delegation to the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) meetings.

Grace Tao, a Tesla global vice president, said in a Weibo post on Thursday evening that Musk had flown to Shanghai specially, even though he was spending only 24 hours in China, and quoted him: "I wanted to come to Shanghai to thank the Asia-Pacific and China teams," Lianhe Zaobao reported. She said he talked with employees about the latest work on driver-assistance technology and humanoid robots, sharing news that "got everyone very excited," and praised the plant's manufacturing and the Chinese team's execution. He described Shanghai as a model of innovation and efficiency and urged staff to set aside short-term speculative thinking and "do things that truly matter."

"Let's make the future look like the future," Musk told the team, according to posts from the event that he shared on X, Teslarati reported. It was his first visit to the factory since 2023, Reuters reported. The plant, which sources 98% of its parts in China, delivered 810,889 Model 3 and Model Y vehicles in the first ten months of the year, up 21.4%. Of the 67,829 vehicles it shipped in October, 59,012 were exports, Teslarati noted.

Most of that output now leaves China. Tesla's retail sales in China fell 66% from a year earlier in October, the lowest since July 2022, according to CnEVPost figures cited by Teslarati, and Chinese owners are still waiting for a full rollout of Tesla's supervised self-driving software. Musk said a year ago he hoped for full approval in China around early 2026, but Tesla has not announced a launch date.

Cybercab arrives in Dallas

"Howdy Dallas. Cybercab has arrived in your city," Tesla's Robotaxi account posted at 11:47 a.m. EST Thursday. Dallas is the second US city after Austin to get the two-seat car, which has no steering wheel or pedals; riders book it in the Tesla Robotaxi app, and Tesla has run unsupervised Model Y robotaxis in Dallas since April, Teslarati reported. Tesla's AI software chief Ashok Elluswamy had named Dallas as next on the Oct. 21 earnings call. Tesla also serves riders with Model Y robotaxis in Houston, Fort Worth, Tampa, San Antonio, Orlando and Miami, InsideEVs reported.

Tesla priced the Cybercab at $29,990 before taxes with a required $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription for the select customers it has invited to buy one, with deliveries targeted for December, Teslarati reported.

Torment II on an AI4 computer

"Tesla's AI, running on our little AI4 computer, can now reach Torment II in Diablo," Musk posted at 5:34 a.m. EST Friday. On Oct. 10 he had written that Digital Optimus, the on-screen agent Tesla is building with SpaceXAI, could play "about halfway through the Diablo campaign so far just by looking at the screen like a human," Tesla News Wire reported. The agent watches live screen video and answers with keyboard and mouse actions, and Musk has said it would run mostly on AI4, the chip already in Tesla's cars. Friday's post came without video or test details, PANews noted.

II · SpaceX

"We are not trying to be NASA"

The Philippine president says his country is in early talks with SpaceX about rocket launch sites, pitching its place on the edge of the Pacific.

Asked in Shenzhen on Friday about reports that Musk was developing two Philippine islands, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said, "I don't have any details on it. I don't think there are any details on it," according to the presidential transcript. "We are just beginning any kind of discussions thus far. But what is happening is he is expanding the launch sites for SpaceX. And maybe the Philippines can look into it."

"We are not trying to be NASA. We are not trying to send a man to the moon or to Mars. What we are trying to do is to get into the business of launch sites."
Ferdinand Marcos Jr. · Shenzhen, Friday

Rockets launch eastward, Marcos said, and because the Philippines sits on the western edge of the Pacific, "should there be a problem, it will fall into the sea. It will not fall on populated areas." He added, "That's why we are talking to SpaceX. We're hoping that Elon Musk will take it up." The country's planned rocket launch in Cagayan "is not the beginning of a space program. It is the beginning of a business that will attract launches," he said, and launching from home would let the Philippines send up its own satellites more cheaply, the Philippine News Agency reported. No sites, investment or timetable were announced, Daily Tribune reported.

The Philippine Space Agency's first demonstration rocket, with South Korea's Perigee Aerospace, is targeted for Feb. 14, 2027, from Cagayan North International Airport, and the country's MULA Earth-observation satellite could fly as early as April 2027 on SpaceX's Transporter-20 rideshare, Tech Pilipinas reported.

Colonies on a substrate

Fermat's Library recalled on Thursday evening how Princeton physicist Gerard O'Neill, after the Moon landings, asked his students how to build colonies in space, and "envisioned giant rotating cylinders with mountains, rivers, valleys and even a blue sky, built almost entirely from material mined on the Moon."

Musk replied at 10:17 p.m. EST: "Better to build colonies on a substrate, like the Moon or Mars, but this will be one of the later approaches."

III · Building in America

Permitting reform: "Sounds promising"

The Senate passed its biggest energy bill since 2022, and a Nashville airport deal clears the way for a Loop station steps from the terminal.

The Senate voted 62-33 on Thursday night to pass the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, which would speed up environmental reviews with tighter deadlines, accelerate electricity transmission and limit federal agencies' power to revoke existing permits, NBC News reported. Fourteen Democrats, including Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, voted for it, The Washington Post reported. It was written by Sens. Shelley Moore Capito (R-W.Va.), Sheldon Whitehouse (D-R.I.), Mike Lee (R-Utah) and Martin Heinrich (D-N.M.). Politico called it the most significant energy legislation to clear the Senate since the Inflation Reduction Act, passed as AI-driven power demand climbs; it now goes to the House before Democrats take control of both chambers in January.

"VICTORY!!!! The Senate just passed the most significant permitting-reform bill in U.S. history," Lee posted at 11:40 p.m. EST, adding that once it becomes law "it'll save American families thousands of dollars a year." Musk replied at 5:27 a.m. EST Friday: "Sounds promising." Capito, who chairs the Environment and Public Works Committee, urged House leaders to pass it "expeditiously" in a statement.

A Loop station 22 feet from the terminal

"The Boring Company and Metropolitan Nashville Airport Authority have signed a 40-year agreement for a Music City Loop station," the company posted at 5:06 p.m. EST Thursday, promising "less than 10 minutes from downtown to a station located 22 feet from the terminal entrance." Musk replied "Cool." Signed Nov. 18, the license has the Boring Company paying all construction, operating and maintenance costs, plus a yearly fee starting at $300,000 and rising 3% a year and $5 for each passenger pickup or drop-off, Aviation Pros reported. Two tunnel-boring machines are working under downtown Nashville and a third is being readied, with an early-2027 launch targeted but no opening date set.

Airport officials project about $343 million in license and passenger fees over a potential 50-year term, counting two optional five-year extensions, LOOP Nashville reported. Doug Kreulen, the airport authority's president and CEO, has said the station would put riders about 22 feet from the terminal door, against a roughly 600-foot walk for Uber and Lyft passengers, LOOP Nashville reported.

IV · Grok, X & AI

"An entire company made of Grok Bots"

Musk pitched SpaceXAI's always-on agents as a whole workforce, and X switched every XChat conversation to encryption on Friday.

"I wake up in the morning, and Grok Bot has already opened multiple PRs, run the tests, and merged them. Having Grok Bot is like hiring a team that never sleeps," X user @texas_twins_dad wrote on Thursday morning, referring to pull requests, the code changes a developer submits for review. Musk quoted it at 12:56 p.m. EST: "You can create an entire company made of Grok Bots!" The post drew more than 21,000 likes.

"You have to be on X"

"It's pretty clear that if you want to have any meaningful conversation about tech you have to be on X," @iterintellectus posted on Thursday. Musk quoted it at 6:19 a.m. EST Friday with one word: "Yes."

Quick Hit

NAND prices. Zero Hedge posted that NAND flash memory prices "have been declining since Q2 2026 and are currently in freefall." Musk replied "Interesting" at 11:56 a.m. EST Thursday.

XChat goes all-encrypted

Friday is the day X's messaging service ends support for its older group chats. "All unencrypted conversations will become encrypted, and group members not on XChat by Friday will need to sign up for XChat to be reincluded," the @chat account said on Nov. 18, as CryptoPanic noted.

V · Interviews & Appearances

On the delegation bus, and on a studio's shareholder list

Musk traveled with the US delegation to APEC in Shenzhen, while at DealBook, David Ellison confirmed Musk is now a shareholder in Skydance.

APEC Leaders' Week, Shenzhen (Nov. 18-19). Musk was filmed on the bus carrying the US delegation as President Donald Trump's motorcade headed to his hotel on Wednesday, Lianhe Zaobao reported; US officials had said he would accompany Trump but not join the meeting of the Chinese, US and Russian leaders. On Thursday he met Ren Hongbin, chairman of the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade, before flying to Shanghai.

Skydance: "an honor and a privilege"

David Ellison, chairman and CEO of Skydance, the company formed by the merger of Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery, confirmed at The New York Times' DealBook Summit that Musk has invested in the company, though not the amount, Variety reported; Semafor had reported in October that it was about $1 billion. "He is, from my perspective, the greatest entrepreneur of our time, and he doesn't invest in a lot of companies," Ellison said. "To have his vote of confidence and to have him as a shareholder is an honor and a privilege." Asked whether it signaled a bigger tie-up with Starlink, he said, "there's nothing" right now. Skydance closed its $111 billion merger with Warner Bros. Discovery on Oct. 6 and carries about $80 billion in net debt, Variety reported.
