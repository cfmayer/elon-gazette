The Morning Picture

Cybertruck winds down at Giga Texas next summer to clear the line for Cybercab

In the small hours of Friday, Elon Musk said Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year so the plant can raise Cybercab output. He later told fans the Cybertruck fleet will still be around for a long time. The same overnight stretch brought a South Korea import-market cheer, a "Terafab is moving fast" nod to chip-fab construction at the Texas plant, and two Starlink posts on clinics and classrooms in remote Indonesia.

I · Cybercab & Tesla

Cybertruck makes room for robotaxi production

Musk put a mid-2027 date on winding down Cybertruck builds at Gigafactory Texas, then reassured owners the truck fleet is not disappearing. Separately he cheered Tesla's ninth straight month atop South Korea's imported-car market.

Mid-2027Target window Musk gave for winding down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas
9thStraight month Tesla led South Korea's imported-car registrations in October
9,165Tesla registrations in Korea in October, up 110.7% year over year

At 1:30 a.m. EST Friday, Musk wrote: "Tesla will be winding down production of the Cybertruck at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production." About two and a half hours later he replied to Owen Sparks, who had called the move the end of an era: "True, but the Cybertruck fleet will be around for a long time."

Tesla stays No. 1 in Korea imports

At 1:15 a.m. EST Friday Musk quote-posted DogeDesigner with "Tesla Korea," amplifying the claim that Tesla held first place among South Korea imported brands for a ninth straight month. Businesskorea, citing the Korea Automobile Importers & Distributors Association, reported Tesla registered 9,165 vehicles in October — up sharply from a year earlier — ahead of Mercedes-Benz (6,744) and BMW (5,633). The Model Y Premium led all imported models with 5,501 registrations; Tesla's Model 3 added 2,199.

II · Terafab

"Terafab is moving fast"

Musk amplified a report that Tesla's chip-fab push at Gigafactory Texas is advancing at "Tesla speed," with a related Fab Lab rising from bare ground to steel in days.

Fab LabRelated build at Giga Texas that Herbert Ong said went from bare ground to steel in days
MidnightWhen Brian (FutureAzA) reportedly still saw the site lit and dusty
3:25 a.m.EST Friday when Musk quote-posted "Terafab is moving fast"

At 3:25 a.m. EST Friday, Musk wrote "Terafab is moving fast," quoting Herbert Ong. Ong said Tesla's chip effort is moving at Tesla speed rather than semiconductor speed, that Musk has said TeraFab is going well, and that FutureAzA watched the related Fab Lab at Giga Texas go from bare ground to steel in days — with Brian seeing the site still lit and dusty at midnight as work continued.

III · Starlink

Clinics and classrooms in remote Indonesia

Musk posted "Starlink saves lives" over a report of a clinic serving ten remote islands, then "Starlink enables education in remote areas" as Katingan's government distributed kits to schools.

10Remote islands DogeDesigner said Sailus Health Center serves in Pangkep, Indonesia
KatinganCentral Kalimantan regency that handed Starlink kits to PAUD, elementary and junior-high schools
Oct 12Date of the Katingan government handover ceremony for school Starlink kits

At 11:36 a.m. EST Thursday, Musk wrote "Starlink saves lives," quoting DogeDesigner's report that Pangkep's local government equipped Sailus Health Center with Starlink so staff can send patient referrals online and coordinate care across about ten remote islands in Indonesia.

Starlink kits for Katingan schools

At 4:32 a.m. EST Friday he followed with "Starlink enables education in remote areas," quoting X Freeze on kits going to remote classrooms. The Katingan regency government said on Oct. 12 that it is distributing satellite internet kits to PAUD, elementary and junior-high schools, plus education coordinators, in areas where conventional connectivity is weak, with activation training and a formal handover.

Quick Hit

Airlines. Musk answered "True" at 11:25 a.m. EST Thursday to an X Freeze post arguing Starlink is becoming a game changer for airline customer satisfaction and loyalty.

IV · Compute & Culture

Iain M. Banks, Seldon, and solar AI in orbit

Musk praised Iain M. Banks's Culture vision of compute abundance, nodded to Hari Seldon's psychohistory on civilization life cycles, and agreed space may be the cheapest place to run superintelligence within a few years.

At 12:11 p.m. EST Thursday, Musk wrote "Banks arguably has the best vision for the future," quoting Yun-Ta Tsai on Iain M. Banks's Culture novels: a civilization of compute abundance where each General System Vehicle is a sandbox for different ideologies and citizens move freely between ships. With enough compute, Tsai argued, autonomy is backed by launching another environment rather than forcing a single trajectory.

"Banks arguably has the best vision for the future"

Seldon and psychohistory

At 3:58 a.m. EST Friday, Musk quote-posted X Freeze with "Seldon, Psychohistory," amplifying a clip in which he describes civilizations cycling like organisms — from early stages through aging — and notes that no civilization lasts forever; the question is how long the inevitable can be delayed.

Quick Hit

AI in orbit. Musk answered "Yes" at 4:34 a.m. EST Friday to an X Freeze summary that he sees space becoming the lowest-cost place to run superintelligence within two to three years via solar-powered orbital data centers.

Quick Hit

Texas permits. Musk called "Interesting" a Gavin Baker note that Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa said currently permitted datacenters may finish construction, covering essentially 2027 and likely well into 2028.

Takeaways

  1. Musk said Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas winds down around mid-2027 to raise Cybercab output, while the truck fleet remains for a long time.
  2. Tesla kept the No. 1 imported-brand slot in South Korea for a ninth month in October, with 9,165 registrations and Model Y Premium leading imports.
  3. He called Terafab "moving fast," amplifying reports of a Fab Lab at Giga Texas rising from bare ground to steel in days.
  4. Two Starlink posts highlighted a remote Indonesian clinic and Katingan schools receiving satellite kits for digital learning.
  5. Lighter notes included praise for Iain M. Banks's Culture, a Seldon/psychohistory nod, and agreement that orbital solar AI data centers look like a near-term cost win.
