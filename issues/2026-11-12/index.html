The Morning Picture

"That would be an honor": Musk would hire Jensen Huang

As Nvidia's board met Wednesday night to decide whether to remove its co-founder and chief executive, Elon Musk answered a call to "hire Jensen" with "That would be an honor," his most-liked post of the day, and agreed that the board should wait for its own investigation. He also gave Tesla's Cybertruck a few more months, extending production through the end of summer 2027 for reasons he called sentimental, cheered Robotaxi's move to round-the-clock service and a self-driving approval in Finland, launched Grok Bot 1.0 as an AI "colleague," and put numbers on SpaceX's power ambitions: a terawatt of solar a year from Earth, a petawatt from the Moon, and clusters that are "actually usable" in two months.

I · Nvidia

Musk sides with Huang as Nvidia's board weighs his future

A second reported affair turned a board that backed its founder on Sunday into one meeting to decide his fate, days before quarterly results.

8%+Nvidia's slide in extended trading after the Journal's report
11Directors, a majority of whom can remove the CEO
Nov. 17Date Nvidia is due to report quarterly results

At 6:22 p.m. EST Wednesday the market commentary account Zero Hedge posted four words: "Elon please hire Jensen." Two hours later Musk answered:

"That would be an honor"
Elon Musk · 8:22 p.m. EST Wednesday

The reply drew more than 108,000 likes by early Thursday, the most of anything he posted during the day.

The board meets

Nvidia's board was meeting late Wednesday to decide whether to oust Jensen Huang, a source told Reuters, RTÉ reported, a sharp shift from Sunday, when the board expressed confidence in him. Earlier Wednesday the New York Times reported that Huang had had an extramarital affair with Kimberly Powell, an Nvidia executive who reported to him. He was already under investigation over his relationship with a businesswoman, Alice Huang, who is alleged to be a Chinese spy; in a memo to employees on Sunday he acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and took "full responsibility."

The Wall Street Journal reported at 5:44 p.m. EST that the company was preparing to fire him, and the stock slid more than 8% in extended trading. Under Nvidia's bylaws a majority of its 11 directors can remove Huang "with or without cause," and all but Huang are considered independent. Huang, 63, owns about 3.6% of the company and has no obvious successor. No decision had been announced as of early Thursday, Axios reported, adding that the White House was read in and that analyst Dan Ives said Nvidia was worth 25% less without Huang.

"Wait for the results of the investigation"

Investor Gavin Baker argued at 6:35 p.m. EST that the board should wait for its inquiry "and/or have a shareholder vote after the investigation is complete," noting the stock was "down 8% after hours on the WSJ report." Musk replied "Exactly" at 8:51 p.m. A minute earlier he had signaled agreement, with an emoji, to Baker's follow-up: "For all of the large shareholders and partners calling me to say that Nvidia's board should wait for the results of the investigation before ousting Jensen: I agree. So don't call me, call people who can impact the decision."

II · SpaceX & Compute

"A terawatt a year from Earth, a petawatt from the Moon"

In a string of replies Musk argued that power in orbit is worth eight times its rating on the ground, and that SpaceX turns clusters into working compute faster than anyone.

8 to 1Musk's ratio: 100 GW in space is roughly 800 GW on Earth
~2 monthsTime SpaceX needs to get a large cluster working reliably
41Gas turbines in the Southaven, Mississippi, air permit

At 8:47 a.m. EST Wednesday a user who posts as vittorio (@IterIntellectus) marveled at SpaceX's solar plans: "100 GW of solar capacity every year," which at full output could generate "a fifth of all the electricity the US uses annually." Musk raised the stakes at 2:05 p.m.:

"Actually, we're aiming for 1TW/year from Earth, rising to over 1PW/year from the Moon. That's why I say that if we achieve this outcome, SpaceX will obviously be worth more than Earth, because space is so staggeringly enormous!"
Elon Musk · 2:05 p.m. EST Wednesday

A terawatt (TW) is 1,000 gigawatts (GW); a petawatt (PW) is 1,000 terawatts.

Quick Hit

Southaven permit stands. Mississippi's Environmental Quality Permit Board denied an appeal and upheld the air permit for 41 gas turbines at the Southaven power plant that serves the Colossus 2 data center, Action News 5 reported. Musk replied to a fan account's summary: "Much appreciated, Mississippi" (emoji omitted).

Why orbit beats the ground

When another user noted that "China installed 315 GW of solar in 2025," Musk replied at 3:22 p.m. EST: "100GW in space is roughly equal to 800GW on Earth. Moreover, solar on Earth must be paired with massive batteries to continue powering compute at night and supplemented with combustion or nuclear power during winter. These are not the same at all."

"Actually usable" compute

Zero Hedge shared a Bernstein ranking of how much of each developer's data-center pipeline is credible, led by Meta, Microsoft, Google, QTS and Vantage. Musk replied at 2:01 p.m. EST: "SpaceX has a chance of going about half the GW next year of actually usable compute. 'Actually usable' is an important metric. It takes us ~2 months working 24/7 to get a large compute cluster working reliably. I'd guess closer to 6 months for others."

"Happy to take the compute back"

Meeting notes posted Wednesday by a semiconductor analyst said Anthropic told sell-side analysts it would not extend its SpaceX compute contract beyond the middle of 2027; neither company confirmed the remarks, DailyMech reported. Anthropic's amended IPO filing says it agreed to spend "up to $120.5 billion" over terms extending into 2029, and that after non-cancelable periods ending in January and March 2027 either side may cancel on 90 days' notice. Replying to a user who read the remark as an answer to an analyst's hypothetical, Musk wrote at 12:40 p.m. EST: "We would be happy to take the compute back. No problem. But I think the opposite is going to happen."

Booster 22 heads to the pad

SpaceX said Tuesday that Booster 22, "the first Super Heavy V3 booster planned to return to the launch site for catch," had moved to the pad at Starbase after catch testing that included "a suspended squeeze test at catch height." Musk shared it at 1:52 p.m. EST Wednesday, and six minutes later posted two photos of a booster's steel flank beside a worker in a lift basket, captioned "Person for scale."

Quick Hit

Data centers and budgets. When a user predicted that "every city / state that permits datacenter construction will have a fiscal surplus, and those that don't will not," Musk replied "True" at 10:05 p.m. EST Wednesday.

III · Tesla

The Cybertruck gets until the end of summer 2027

Five days after announcing the pickup's end, Musk added a few months of production to work through orders, and said profit was not the reason.

At 12:11 p.m. EST Wednesday Musk wrote: "In order to work through more of the Cybertruck order backlog, production will be extended through the end of summer 2027." Twenty minutes later he added:

"This is not the smartest move from a profit-maximizing standpoint btw, but there must be some room in this world for sentimentality or what's the point?"
Elon Musk · 12:32 p.m. EST Wednesday

On Nov. 6 Musk said Tesla would wind down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas around the middle of 2027 to make room for more Cybercab output, Tesla Mag recounted; on Nov. 9 he warned the line would shut "in about 8 months" and that Tesla would "have to return deposits for a lot of people," CBS News reported. The extension now runs production through most of September, giving the truck a roughly 10-month runway, InsideEVs wrote; Tesla has sold some 70,000 since deliveries began at the end of 2023, per industry estimates it cited. Musk gave no exact end date and did not say whether deposits will still be returned.

Robotaxi works around the clock

At 11:31 p.m. EST Wednesday Tesla's Robotaxi account announced: "Robotaxi now runs 24/7. Every city we're available in." Musk quoted it at 12:12 a.m. Thursday: "Robotaxi works 168 hours a week." The change closes the overnight gap left by a 5 a.m. to 2 a.m. schedule, TeslaNorth reported, and meets a target AI software chief Ashok Elluswamy set on Tesla's October earnings call to unlock 24/7 operation before the end of 2026. The service runs in Texas in Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio. Musk also quoted a Robotaxi video titled "Lofi beats to self-drive to" with "Chill vibes."

Self-driving approved in Finland

Tesla Europe posted at 1:18 a.m. EST Wednesday that its supervised Full Self-Driving (FSD) is "now approved in Finland," with "rollout begins soon" (emoji omitted). Musk amplified it at 12:11 p.m.: "Tesla self-driving now approved in Finland." He laughed at a joke from Yun-Ta Tsai quoting a Finnish friend: "The car now drives itself. The driver still sits in complete silence and doesn't talk to anyone. So nothing really changed."

Megapacks and the grid

Tesla's Megapack account explained Tuesday how its Controller-Hardware-In-the-Loop (CHIL) testing simulates a whole battery site in real time before grid connection, and said CHIL validated 444 grid-forming Megapacks at the Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub, "delivering 600 MW / 1.6 GWh of storage months ahead of schedule." Musk quoted it at 1:49 p.m. EST Wednesday: "Tesla Megapacks are designed to ensure grid stability, so that you never have a blackout." The hub in Plumpton, west of Melbourne, can power 200,000 homes and was completed in November 2025, according to its co-owner, Victoria's State Electricity Commission.

"Optimus Academy"

Tesla commentator Farzad Mesbahi (@farzyness) posted an 18-minute video, "Why Tesla Is Building A School For Robots," at 3:08 p.m. EST Wednesday. Musk replied with a name for it at 1:12 a.m. Thursday: "Optimus Academy."

Quick Hit

The snake, 11 years on. Musk quoted a clip of his 2015 Late Show appearance, in which he showed Stephen Colbert a robotic snake that plugs itself into a car, with "11 years ago." His deadpan line then, per the post: "For the prototype, at least, I would recommend not dropping anything when you're near it."

IV · Grok & AI

"Hire Grok Bot as a colleague"

SpaceX's AI unit took its workplace agent out of beta as a shared teammate for Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls.

At 1:22 p.m. EST Wednesday the Grok Bot account introduced version 1.0: "AI teammates that work alongside your whole team." Musk quoted it six minutes later:

"Hire Grok @Bot as a colleague and give it your hardest tasks!"
Elon Musk · 1:28 p.m. EST Wednesday

The launch post lists three changes since the beta: Bots can be shared and brought into Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls, learning from everyone they work with; they can spot work that needs doing and offer to take it on; and they are faster and more reliable in the browser, with better documents and slide decks. For shared spaces, the company says, Bots respect each person's permissions, ask before taking sensitive actions and check whether information is appropriate to share in a public channel. Team Bots require Team or Enterprise plans; Meetings come with Team, Enterprise and Ultra plans.

"The best AI agent orchestrator"

Entrepreneur Greg Isenberg (@gregisenberg) wrote at 2:08 p.m. EST that "grok bot is the BEST AI agent orchestrator right now," sharing a setup that runs other tools for engineering and design. Musk quoted him at 12:59 a.m. Thursday: "Try Grok @Bot."

"Still in the first mile"

Replying to a long post arguing that the first lab to reach artificial general intelligence could pull permanently ahead, and that SpaceX and Tesla should pour every resource into catching up, Musk wrote at 4:21 a.m. EST Thursday: "The road ahead is long and we are still in the first mile, although it may seem otherwise." The post he answered had ended: "It's do or die."

On the wire

Two months versus six. Musk put SpaceX's time to get a large compute cluster working reliably at about two months of round-the-clock work, against his guess of closer to six months for others, in a reply to Zero Hedge.
