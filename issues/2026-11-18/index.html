The Morning Picture

A facepalm for the FAA, then a motorcade in Shenzhen

Elon Musk answered Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's defense of a ground-based air traffic network with a facepalm on Tuesday, then reposted the warning he wrote in February 2025 about the Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) failing communications. Tesla unveiled Powercore, a solid-state transformer that Musk called "advanced solid state transformers at scale," and he said the product had been planned for more than three years. He pitched Grok Bot as an accountant and a cost cutter, said Grok 4.8 still needs "another week or two of polish," and told followers that SpaceX is "assuming zero sales to OpenAI." Overnight he explained the literary names of The Boring Company's tunnel machines and put the price of cities on the Moon and Mars at "about a megaton per year to orbit." By Wednesday evening in China he was riding in President Donald Trump's motorcade in Shenzhen.

I · Starlink

"Our airspace is very big"

Duffy explained why the FAA passed on free Starlink terminals; Musk answered with an emoji and an old post.

43,000+Likes on Musk's facepalm reply
$50 billionDuffy's ask for a new air traffic system

Asked at a press conference on Tuesday about Musk's claim that free Starlinks had been offered and turned down, Duffy said the department had tested "one of its prior iterations" at its tech lab and "there was some issues that we had to navigate," Mixed Times reported. "We thought that the best method is to have a network that's based on the ground. Now, I think what he's doing with Starlink and the speed is remarkable, but, our airspace is very big. We have assets everywhere. And so, again, we used him for different parts of the airspace." He said Starlink had been "a great solution for some of our assets in Alaska."

Eric Daugherty posted the clip at 3:39 p.m. EST. Musk quoted it at 4:55 p.m. with nothing but a facepalm emoji, a reply that drew more than 43,000 likes; Benzinga called it a facepalm moment. At 8:26 p.m. he answered "Good question" to a post by Nick Sortor that pointed to Duffy's Alaska remark and asked, "Why can't we spread it here?"

"I wrote this 18 months ago"

Just after midnight Musk reposted his own post from Feb. 27, 2025, with the line "I wrote this 18 months ago." The original read:

"To be clear here, the Verizon communication system to air traffic control is breaking down very rapidly. The FAA assessment is single digit months to catastrophic failure, putting air traveler safety at serious risk. The Starlink terminals are being sent at NO COST to the taxpayer on an emergency basis to restore air traffic control connectivity. The situation is extremely dire."
Elon Musk · 10:09 a.m. EST, Feb. 27, 2025

The repost drew more than 18,000 likes; Benzinga described it as Musk doubling down. When the account Chesschick01 wrote that "Elon predicted the ground stop a year ago," Musk replied at 5:45 a.m. EST Wednesday: "Yeah."

The FBI joins the router inquiry

Duffy also said the FBI is helping investigate Friday's failure. "We've been able to recreate what happened," he said. "The root cause, we don't have clarity yet." The router began flooding the network when it was switched on during a copper-to-fiber upgrade at a remote site, on the 69th router change of its type, Right Line News reported; Duffy has raised his request to Congress to roughly $50 billion. He has said he wants to move away from L3Harris, the contractor that owns the network, but "I can't cancel the network that they own," Mixed Times noted. Verizon won the FAA's network contract in March 2023, valued at $2.4 billion over 15 years if every option is exercised. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford told lawmakers in July 2025 that the agency "would not use Starlink (or any satellite service) as the sole communications technology" for safety-critical services.

Quick Hit

"Starlink saves lives." Musk quoted a post saying a Starlink connection let four sailors talk to New Zealand's rescue center after a whale strike sank their yacht. The Australian yacht Tai Tam went down about 250 nautical miles north of Norfolk Island in late September, and the cargo ship Sofrana Surville picked all four up from their life raft, gCaptain reported.

II · Tesla

"Advanced solid state transformers at scale"

Tesla's energy team introduced Powercore, a transformer, inverter and breaker in one box, and Musk said it had been years in the works.

"Introducing Powercore," the Tesla Megapack account posted at 11:00 a.m. EST Tuesday: a "solid state medium voltage transformer, inverter and breaker," rated at "7.4 MW/9 MVA continuously at 34.5 kVac w/ grid-forming support for 10MVA of peak power. Made in America. Operating 2027." Musk quoted it at 11:46 a.m. with "Advanced solid state transformers at scale from Tesla," a post that drew more than 19,000 likes.

Tesla's follow-up spec sheet lists an AC range of 10 to 34.5 kilovolts, a DC range of 800 to 2,000 volts, peak efficiency of 98.86%, a warranty of up to 25 years and an operating range of minus 40 to 55 degrees Celsius, Electrek reported. The unit holds no flammable oil, its built-in solid-state breaker clears faults up to 1,000 times faster than a conventional one, and Tesla pitches it for battery storage, utility-scale solar and 800-volt DC data centers. Tesla has not given pricing, customers or a factory location.

AJ Booth, Tesla's head of energy and charging, told pv magazine USA that the breaker can interrupt a fault in 100 microseconds: "We think we're safer because we can interrupt faults a thousand times faster than traditional equipment." The magazine noted that shortages of transformer inputs have stretched lead times to as long as four years.

"Planned for 3+ years"

Whole Mars Catalog wrote that the product "seems to compete directly with former Tesla executive Drew Baglino's startup Heron Power." Musk replied at 12:54 p.m. EST: "No, this has been planned for 3+ years." Heron Power plans to begin volume production of its own solid-state transformer, the Heron Link, in 2027, the same year Powercore is due, Electrek noted; Tesla first said it would build its own transformers in September 2025, when it unveiled Megapack 3 and Megablock.

III · SpaceX & The Boring Company

"About a megaton per year to orbit"

Musk sized up what Moon and Mars cities will take, cheered a Flight 15 booster shot, and named the poems behind his tunnel machines.

Replying to Sequoia partner Shaun Maguire, who had posted an image under the words "Insane stat!", Musk wrote at 4:53 a.m. EST Wednesday:

"About a megaton per year to orbit is needed to build self-sustaining cities on the Moon and Mars."
Elon Musk · 4:53 a.m. EST Wednesday

The figure echoes the math he posted in January 2020: a Starship design goal of three flights a day on average at more than 100 tons a flight, so that "every 10 ships yield 1 megaton per year to orbit," Space.com reported.

Booster B22 on the way down

Tim Dodd of Everyday Astronaut shared a tracking shot from his Flight 15 "Super Cut," filmed by Consider Cosmos: "Watch sound bend light as Super Heavy Booster B22 reenters the atmosphere. This is maybe one of my all-time favorite shots I've ever seen of a rocket." Musk quoted it at 10:42 p.m. EST Tuesday: "Nice shot."

"Take them to see a Starship launch"

Investor Gavin Baker was quoted in a clip saying, "If you have children, if you have grandchildren, take them to see a Starship launch. It will change their life." Musk agreed at 8:55 p.m. EST: "True, Starship launches are incredibly exciting."

Godot, Line-Storm and Prufrock

"The machines of @boringcompany were named after: Waiting for Godot, A Line-storm Song, The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock," Musk posted at 4:36 a.m. EST Wednesday, naming Samuel Beckett's play and poems by Robert Frost and T. S. Eliot. At 6:05 a.m. he added that "Prufrock is perfectly named," and to a reader who noted that Waiting for Godot is a play about waiting indefinitely, he replied: "We waited for what felt like an eternity for that first machine."

He also reposted, with the single word "Prufrock," an X Freeze post saying Prufrock-MB3 has arrived in Nashville as the third machine for the Music City Loop, that Prufrock-MB4 is expected within eight weeks, and that the company is holding a hiring event on Tuesday, Nov. 24. The third machine is poised to launch from a site on Murfreesboro Pike, the Nashville Business Journal reported, adding, citing The Tennessean, that the first two have combined to travel some 6,000 feet underground. The company's own October update set a goal of four machines underground before the end of the year; the first section of the airport-to-downtown loop is due to open in early 2027, Equipment World reported.

IV · X, Grok & AI

"Grok @Bot can do your accounting"

Musk sold Grok Bot as a back-office worker, put a date range on Grok 4.8 and took a shot at OpenAI's demand.

"Five Bots now have full-time roles on our accounting team. We onboard, train, and manage them just like human teammates," the @bot account posted on Tuesday. Musk quoted it at 10:15 p.m. EST: "Grok @Bot can do your accounting."

SpaceXSI's write-up describes five Team Bots for provisioning, billing, billing support, cash applications and collections, each owning a job end to end and bringing in a person "when it encounters something that requires human judgment or approval." Billing Bot cut the time to issue an invoice "from roughly 24 hours to minutes," and Cash Applications Bot does in about 30 minutes reconciliation work that "ordinarily takes an accountant three to four hours." Refunds still require human approval.

Earlier, at 8:59 p.m., Musk wrote over a summary of the same story: "Grok @Bot can save you millions in corporate expenses! It will research every expenditure, evaluate its necessity and find better deals on the Internet or by calling suppliers on the phone directly."

Grok 4.8: "another week or two of polish"

An account pointed out that Musk had joked early Tuesday about "waiting for Grok 4.8" while the Grok chatbot told a user a soft rollout was under way. Musk replied at 12:56 p.m. EST: "Grok 4.8 has not rolled out for general use yet, just some trials behind the scenes." Half an hour later he added:

"We did do a soft rollout to some Bot users, but still have another week or two of polish before general release."
Elon Musk · 1:29 p.m. EST Tuesday

"Zero sales to OpenAI"

Sam Altman said in a clip posted by Fireside Alpha that chipmakers may be double counting demand, with "many people making chips" assuming "we're going to buy all of their chips," though "the data center build out, that stuff is solid." Musk replied at 8:08 a.m. EST Tuesday: "SpaceX is assuming zero sales to OpenAI. If OpenAI is not seeing demand rising super fast, then something is wrong with their products." Wccftech noted that SpaceX has described itself as a computing provider since its IPO, with Colossus data centers that have capacity agreements with Google and Anthropic.

Founders or committees

"You can tell from the earnings call whether the company is led by founders or by committees," Yun-Ta Tsai wrote on Tuesday night, after a Nvidia call that opened with chairman Harvey Jones saying the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," Politico reported. Huang had said he would come back if asked; interim chief executive Colette Kress presented record quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, CNBC reported. Musk replied at 6:21 a.m. EST Wednesday: "So true."

AI influencers and the "white pill"

"Super intelligence will hack your limbic system," Musk wrote at 8:36 p.m. EST Tuesday, quoting a prediction that "in the next 2 years 50%+ of what we watch will come from AI influencers," which introduced Claudia, an AI pop star created by anabology. Early Wednesday he shared Claudia's song "WHITEPILL," pitched as "universal high income, but make it claudia," with the words "Take the White Pill," and called it "Such a jolly song!"

V · Interviews & Appearances

In Shenzhen with the president

Musk arrived in China with the presidential motorcade as APEC leaders gathered, with a DealBook appearance on screen still to come.

President Trump flew into Shenzhen on the afternoon of Nov. 18 for the APEC leaders' meeting, and when his motorcade reached the city's Langham hotel that evening, Musk was among the party, The Paper reported at 7:13 p.m. local time, 6:13 a.m. EST (translated). He is the main chief executive traveling with the president but will not join the Nov. 19 meeting of Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, according to Reuters and CBS News accounts gathered by GSM Global.

"Is the Thucydides Trap ever avoidable?"

Less than half an hour after the motorcade report, at 6:38 a.m. EST, Musk posted a question about the idea that a rising power and an established one are drawn toward war:

"Is the Thucydides Trap ever avoidable? I wish it were, but, inevitably, the most interesting outcome is the most likely."
Elon Musk · 6:38 a.m. EST Wednesday

DealBook, remotely

Musk is listed among the speakers at the New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, Nov. 18; he told Andrew Ross Sorkin he would be in China, "so it will be digital me," TeslaNorth reported. The summit's main-stage program begins at 9 a.m. EST, according to CNBC.
