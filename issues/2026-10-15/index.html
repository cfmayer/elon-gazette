The Morning Picture

Starshield slowdown, "Time for Zelensky to go," and the Roadster tonight

Ukraine said SpaceX restricted military Starshield terminals on moving vehicles overnight into Wednesday. Elon Musk replied that Starshield limits are "decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS," clashed again with Poland's foreign minister and, late Wednesday, posted that it was "Time for Zelensky to go."

Tonight at 8:55 p.m. EDT, Tesla streams its new Roadster reveal, which Musk calls "a candidate for greatest product demo of all time." His roughly $366 million in midterm giving is due in a federal filing today, according to The Guardian. On X he pushed Grok Bot and the new Grok Voice 3.0, and said SpaceX will make users whole if Grok Bot errs with a purchase.

I · Politics & Ukraine

"Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military"

A restriction on Starshield terminals in motion slowed Ukrainian drones and drew a rebuke from Warsaw.

Starshield

"On the night of October 14, without prior notice to the Defense Forces, SpaceX unilaterally restricted the operation of Starshield terminals while in motion," a Ukrainian defense ministry official said, Politico reported. Musk replied to Ukraine's Mykhailo Fedorov at 3 p.m. EDT Wednesday: "Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government. Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS." Commercial Starlink, he wrote, "is limited to civilian or defensive activities." Two US officials told CNN the move was not a high-level US policy decision.

"Hey, @elonmusk, big man, are you playing politics with your products again? As your European customers, we are watching you," Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski posted, Interfax-Ukraine reported. Musk later quoted a post on Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky with "And so the slaughter continues … Time for Zelensky to go" (post) and wrote "Exactly what I predicted has happened" on a post recalling his 2022 peace proposal.

The donations filing

The numbers behind Musk's roughly $366 million in midterm giving "will be released in a federal filing on Thursday," The Guardian reported. The election is November 3.

II · Tesla

Roadster tonight, 8:55 p.m. EDT

"Probably metaphorically, though some chance literally."

Tesla streams the new Roadster's reveal tonight at 7:55 p.m. Central, 8:55 p.m. EDT, on its X account; the invite-only event near Waco, Texas, was moved from October 1 because of the weather. "The new Tesla Roadster is a candidate for greatest product demo of all time," Musk posted, then added: "Let's just say that this demo will be a BANGER, probably metaphorically, though some chance literally." Tesla first showed the car in 2017 and has not delivered one, Electrek noted.

Quoting a post that gasoline-only cars had fallen below half of global new-vehicle sales, Musk wrote: "Major threshold passed. Almost all cars will be electric in the future." Tesla shares fell 2.15% on Wednesday as a hot inflation report hit technology stocks, AP reported.

III · SpaceX, Grok & X

Grok Voice 3.0, and a guarantee for Grok Bot buyers

Musk promoted SpaceXAI's new voice model and answered Amazon's block on AI shopping agents.

"Grok is the best conversational voice model in the world," Musk wrote, quoting SpaceXAI's introduction of Grok Voice 3.0, billed as "the world's most intelligent speech-to-speech model." After a user said Amazon had cut off a Grok Bot as an "unauthorized AI agent," Musk replied: "There are far more purchasing websites than just Amazon!" He added at 1:57 a.m. EDT: "If Grok @Bot makes a mistake with a purchase or leaks your card (never happened yet), SpaceX will make you whole."

SpaceX shares fell 2.80% as AI stocks slumped, Reuters reported. Cargo Dragon docks at the space station at about 11 a.m. EDT today (NASA), and a Falcon 9 launches the USSF-481 mission from California at 7:44 p.m. EDT (Spaceflight Now). The FAA's review of up to 456 Starship launches a year at Starbase is taking comments through November 30 (Federal Register).
